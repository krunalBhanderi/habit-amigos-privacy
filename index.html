2. What stays on your phone

Habit Amigos works without an account. Everything you keep for yourself is stored in a database on your phone and is not sent to us:

  • your private habits: names, emoji, colours, schedules, targets and reminders;
  • your check-ins, notes, rest days, streaks and freezes for those habits;
  • your app settings, and what the widgets show;
  • any export files you create (JSON or CSV). They are saved where you choose.

Reminders and widgets are produced by your phone from that local data. We cannot see them, read them or recover them for you.

A note about phone backups

Your phone's own backup may copy this data. Android can back up app data to your Google account, and iPhone can include it in an iCloud device backup. Google or Apple do this under your phone's settings and their own privacy policies. We do not receive those copies and cannot see or delete them. It means your habits can come back if you lose or change your phone. If you do not want it, you can turn app backup off in your phone's settings.

Deleting the app removes your private habits from your phone. If your phone's backup includes them, reinstalling the app may restore them.

4. Who else handles your data

We use a few companies to run the service. They handle data on our behalf and under their own terms with us.

  • Google (Firebase and Google Cloud). Sign-in, the database, server functions, push notifications, this website's hosting, crash reports and, if you allow it, analytics. Also "Sign in with Google" and Google Play billing.
  • Apple. "Sign in with Apple", App Store billing and delivery of push notifications to iPhones.
  • RevenueCat, Inc. Manages subscriptions and tells the app whether Pro is active. It receives an anonymous user ID and purchase information from Apple or Google.
  • Crash and usage tools. Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics, both from Google.

We do not give your data to advertisers or data brokers. We may share information when the law requires it, to protect someone's safety, or to enforce our Terms of Use. If our business is ever sold or merged, your data may move to the new owner, who must keep to this policy, and we will tell you first.

6. What we do not do

  • We show no ads and use no advertising SDKs or advertising IDs.
  • We do not sell or rent your data, or share it for advertising.
  • We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites. That is why the iPhone app does not show an "ask app not to track" prompt.
  • We do not read your contacts or photos, and we do not collect your precise location. We only use your time zone.
  • We never send your habit names or notes to analytics or crash tools.

7. How long we keep data

  • Private habits: we do not hold them. They stay on your phone until you delete them or the app.
  • Account and pact data: until you delete your account.
  • If you leave a pact: you appear as "Former member" in its history, and your check-ins stay so the other members' flame and days together stay correct.
  • If you delete your account: we delete your profile, sign-in details, device tokens, check-ins and notes. A pact's day-by-day history (which days were kept and how many members were covered) stays without your name or ID, shown as "Former member", so your friends' flame and days together do not change. A pact with no members left is deleted. To ask for it by email, write to nextgendevls@gmail.com from the address on your account.
  • Invites: they stop working after 7 days or when revoked.
  • Reports: kept only as long as needed to handle the report and to stop repeat abuse.
  • Purchases: Apple, Google and RevenueCat keep purchase records under their own policies and tax law.
  • Crash reports: up to 90 days. Usage statistics: no more than 14 months. Server logs: up to 30 days.
  • Backups: our own backups are purged within 30 days after data is deleted from live systems.

8. Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to show you the data we hold about you, correct it, delete it, or give you a copy. You can do most of this in the app: edit your profile, export your private habits (JSON or CSV), and delete your account. For anything else, email nextgendevls@gmail.com. We may ask you to write from the email address on your account so we know it is you, and we aim to reply within 30 days.

European Economic Area and United Kingdom

You have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, data portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. We do not make decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects. You can complain to your local data protection authority.

India

Under the DPDP Act you have the right to information about your data, to correct and erase it, to have your grievance handled, and to nominate another person to exercise your rights if you die or cannot act for yourself. Our grievance contact is the email address in section 1. We aim to answer within 30 days. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

United States

We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context advertising. Depending on your state, you may have the right to access or delete your data, and we will not treat you differently for asking.

9. Age limits

Using Habit Amigos for your own private habits needs no account, and we collect nothing from you, so there is no age limit for that.

To create or join a pact you need an account, and you must be 16 or older, or 18 or older if you live in India. We do not knowingly create accounts for anyone younger. Where Apple or Google offers an age signal to apps, we may use it for this check. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under the limit, we delete it. If you believe a child has a pact account, email us.

10. Security and international transfers

Data moves between the app and our servers over encrypted connections, and Google encrypts the data it stores for us. Access rules let only the members of a pact read that pact's data. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects you, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

Google, Apple and RevenueCat may process data in countries other than yours, including the United States. Where the law requires it, we rely on safeguards such as the EU Standard Contractual Clauses.
